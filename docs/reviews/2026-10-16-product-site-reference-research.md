# Product-site reference research for cultureQC

Observed 2026-10-16. Method: first-party product pages only, read through retrieved page text and image descriptions. I did not inspect rendered desktop/mobile layouts, motion, loading behavior, or conversion results. The observations below concern content order and stated interactions, not visual quality or measured marketing effectiveness. These products differ substantially in scope from cultureQC.

## Automata — LINQ

[LINQ product page](https://www.automata.tech/linq): Leads with a platform proposition, then a meeting CTA. Its next sections explain why the platform exists, show how orchestration connects hardware, workflows and data, and separate the hardware bench from software and Python SDK. “Any vendor,” “Open API,” and “Any ELN/LIMS” make integration fit visible before deeper feature links. A partner-logo block and a tailored meeting form close the page.

**Transfer:** Give cultureQC a compact picture of its place in the host workflow: captured image → reading and guardrail → action or human review → retained record. Show the real `analyze()` handoff and the fields an integration receives. Let the primary demo illustrate one decision before visitors open API details.

**Do not borrow:** “One platform,” vendor-agnostic breadth, partner logos, or turnkey deployment. cultureQC is a bounded analysis component, and its integration/validation remains setup-specific.

## Benchling — product overview

[Benchling product overview](https://www.benchling.com/product-overview): Opens with a broad scientific-progress proposition and immediate “View demos” / “Contact sales” actions. It then routes visitors to distinct product workflows, including automation framed as an instrument run becoming a scientific decision and entering the scientific record. Later “why” and customer sections supply organizational proof.

**Transfer:** Pair one clear product outcome with a low-friction demo path, then expose deeper evidence routes for scientific and integration reviewers. cultureQC can make “image → decision → record” concrete with its own microscopy and selected record, rather than starting with model names. A short explanation of where a scientist or operator intervenes would help an executive read the demo correctly.

**Do not borrow:** Enterprise-scale trust claims, customer testimony, complete R&D-platform framing, or any claim that cultureQC automatically closes the next lab step. Use its own stored outputs and named limits as proof.

## Molecular Devices — CellXpress.ai

[CellXpress.ai system page](https://www.moleculardevices.com/products/3d-biology/cellxpress-ai-automated-cell-culture-system): Opens with the named system, instrument imagery (as described by the page), “Speak to an expert,” and a brochure action. It next maps monitoring, feeding, scheduling, imaging and decision support into a culture workflow. Image-led outcome blocks, a workflow/video link, capabilities, user-control explanation, FAQs, specifications and an expert CTA follow. Its quantitative time/scale claims carry stated scenario qualifications on the page.

**Transfer:** Put actual culture images and a result state near the opening claim. Demonstrate the meaningful transitions: a reading, a deferred decision, and a re-image request. Place the reason and scope beside each output. Close with the concrete pilot inputs needed from one partner setup; the CTA should lead to a real pilot plan.

**Do not borrow:** Automated feeding/passaging, 24/7 execution, hardware imagery, scale or labor-savings figures, and any implication of autonomous vessel actuation. cultureQC has not established those outcomes.

## Sartorius — Incucyte software

[Incucyte live-cell analysis software page](https://www.sartorius.com/en/products/live-cell-imaging-analysis/live-cell-analysis-software): Leads with acquiring, viewing, analyzing and sharing living-cell images and a software-demo CTA. It describes guided acquisition, visualization, analysis and data management before detailed confluence-analysis workflows and software modules. A vessel view, trends/outliers and metric overlays are described as parts of the actual analysis experience; release updates are placed later in the page.

**Transfer:** Make cultureQC’s own image and computed layer an immediately legible product interaction: select a scenario, see the recommendation and reason, then reveal optional overlays and its exact record. Keep model and release detail available for technical visitors without making version history the main story.

**Do not borrow:** Claims of thousands of wells, long-term acquisition, archive management or broad cell-type coverage. The cited page describes a larger imaging system; cultureQC analyzes one supplied image per call.

## Implications for the v4 site

1. **Lead with independent product value.** Explain the one-image decision-support workflow in plain terms, then let a real stored example prove it. Keep v2/v4 comparison in release notes, not the homepage proposition.
2. **Make the image demonstrative.** The strongest interaction is seeing a selected image, its measurement, the recommended action, why the rule chose it, and when a person takes over. Annotation layers and hashes become inspectable proof at the relevant point.
3. **Put proof beside claims.** Show source, sample, setup, and limitation beside each numerical result; distinguish stored output from a live run and shipped decisions from experimental models.
4. **Show integration fit without pretending full automation.** A small host-system diagram and an exact sample output can make the API-first role tangible. End with an offline one-setup pilot proposal and its required images and labels.
5. **Treat reference sites as patterns, not validation.** Their hierarchy and CTAs demonstrate choices those companies made. They provide no evidence that the same design increases cultureQC’s conversion or technical readiness.
