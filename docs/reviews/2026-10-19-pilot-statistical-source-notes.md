# T10 pilot statistical source notes

Researched 2026-10-19. These are statistical design notes for a proposed, offline/shadow partner evaluation. They do not set partner acceptance thresholds, declare product performance, or replace the partner's protocol approval.

## Findings

### Independent units and clustered observations

- The inferential sample size is the number of independent experimental units relevant to the claim, not the number of image files or extracted fields. Hurlbert defines pseudoreplication as testing treatment effects with unreplicated treatments or replicates that are not statistically independent ([Hurlbert 1984, *Pseudoreplication and the Design of Ecological Field Experiments*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1942661)). For this pilot, fields and repeated frames from a culture are nested observations; they cannot automatically be counted as independent cultures.
- Clustered sample-size methods account for within-cluster correlation, often through a design effect such as `1 + (m − 1) × ICC` under equal cluster size and a simple exchangeable-correlation setup. This is a planning approximation, not a universal correction; multi-level/repeated designs and unequal cluster sizes need matching methods ([Rutterford, Copas & Eldridge 2015, *Methods for sample size determination in cluster randomized trials*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4521133/)).
- **Protocol implication (inference):** name the unit for each claim before counting. For generalization to new biological cultures, culture/vessel is the natural top-level unit; frames, fields, and time points are repeated/nested observations. If multiple cultures share a passage, run, operator, or acquisition session, record those links and consider them in splitting and uncertainty estimation. Hold out whole biological/acquisition groups for the final evaluation; do not split adjacent frames from one culture across development and final test.

### Exact one-sided binomial bounds

- NIST Dataplot defines exact one-sided lower and upper binomial confidence limits and specifies the alpha level ([NIST exact binomial reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbino.htm)). For independent Bernoulli trials, the one-sided 95% upper Clopper–Pearson limit after zero events in `n` trials is `1 − 0.05^(1/n)`; the one-sided 95% lower limit after `n/n` successes is `0.05^(1/n)`.
- The arithmetic checks: **0 events in 299 independent trials** gives upper bound about **0.997%** (at or below 1%); **29/29 successes** gives lower bound about **90.18%** (at or above 90%). These are illustrative boundary calculations, not a recommendation that those counts establish partner readiness.
- The binomial calculations require trials that can reasonably be treated as independent and identically defined Bernoulli units. Repeated frames/visits within a culture, or multiple observations sharing sessions, violate the naive frame-as-trial interpretation. Consequently, 299 frames from a few cultures do not establish a ≤1% event rate, and 29 successful frames do not establish ≥90% episode sensitivity. Use independently defined culture-level episodes where that is the target, or a prespecified cluster-aware interval/model/design with enough independent clusters.

### Precision and sample-size planning

- NIST's sampling guidance says to define the estimand and desired precision first, connect precision to sample size, use prior information for unknown population properties such as variance, and assess practicality; it notes that precision depends on variability, measurement error, independent replications, and sampling efficiency ([NIST sample-size selection](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section3/ppc333.htm), [NIST sampling scheme](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section3/ppc332.htm)). For a mean with known standard deviation, NIST gives the familiar dependence on `σ / √N`; with estimated variance, uncertainty in `s` affects interval width ([NIST confidence limits for the mean](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda352.htm)).
- NIST's sample-size guidance for hypothesis tests also requires a specified detectable difference, type-I error, power, and (for continuous outcomes) variance ([NIST sample sizes for mean comparisons](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc222.htm); [for proportions](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc242.htm)). Thus a claim such as “bounded confluency error” cannot yield a numeric required `n` until the allowable error/precision and a defensible variance source are specified. Likewise, a binary safety/coverage claim needs a defined event, bound/acceptance criterion, confidence level, and independent-unit structure.
- **Defensible draft plan:** predefine image eligibility, the reference confluency annotation method, target-range strata, and the per-culture summaries. Report paired prediction-minus-reference errors by independent culture, including bias, MAE, error distribution/quantiles, and coverage of any claimed uncertainty interval; show results by the predeclared density range. Treat the partner's routine workflow as a paired comparator. Set the allowable error and confidence-interval precision with the partner before evaluating outcomes. Estimate variance/correlation from separate development or blinded internal pilot data, then calculate the needed number of independent cultures using a cluster-aware method or simulation matching the frozen analysis. Inflate for expected unusable cultures without counting their frames as replacements. For binary safety and analyzable-coverage endpoints, specify numerator, denominator, and independent event/culture unit; plan enough independent units for the chosen one-sided confidence bound and use a cluster-aware design if observations remain clustered. Freeze criteria before the untouched test set is scored.
- If no partner-approved margin, target, or credible variance estimate exists yet, leave numeric confluency sample size and pass/fail cutoffs explicitly **TBD**. A preliminary blinded variance-estimation stage can inform a separately frozen confirmation design, but should not be pooled into a final confirmatory test if it influenced thresholds or analysis choices.

## Practical boundary

The 299 and 29 calculations are exact-binomial illustrations only. They do not account for clustering, multiplicity across endpoints, subgroup precision, missing/rejected images, selection of thresholds, or power to detect a useful paired improvement. They are not sufficient sample-size plans for correlated frames or repeated observations. A cluster-aware power/precision analysis needs the partner's chosen estimand and margin, likely cluster counts and sizes, plausible between- and within-culture variation/correlation, missingness assumptions, and frozen analysis; none should be invented from the public sources.
